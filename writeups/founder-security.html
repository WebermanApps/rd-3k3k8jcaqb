Research Dashboard

Founder security brief — 2026-10-14 (Max Weberman / WebermanApps / Smart Upkeep LLC)

Ranked by impact vs effort (highest first).

  1. Password manager + phishing-resistant MFA (passkeys/hardware keys) on email, GitHub, Stripe, domain registrar. Why: account takeover is the #1 path to losing everything. Action: install a password manager; add 2 hardware keys/passkeys to Google + GitHub; turn off SMS fallback. Source: https://www.cisa.gov/MFA ; https://pages.nist.gov/800-63-4/sp800-63b.html
  2. Store recovery codes offline. Why: lost key = locked out of your own company. Action: print GitHub/Google/Stripe recovery codes, keep in a safe. Source: https://docs.github.com/en/authentication/securing-your-account-with-two-factor-authentication-2fa/configuring-two-factor-authentication-recovery-methods
  3. Least-privilege, expiring tokens for AI agents. Why: an agent token with full repo/publish rights is a skeleton key. Action: use fine-grained PATs scoped to single repos, 30–90 day expiry; no org-admin for agents. Source: https://docs.github.com/en/authentication/keeping-your-account-and-data-secure/managing-your-personal-access-tokens
  4. Treat prompt injection as real; human approval before publish/send/delete. Why: agents reading web/email can be hijacked (OWASP LLM01, excessive agency LLM06). Action: agents may draft; you approve pushes to public sites, emails, payments. Source: https://genai.owasp.org/llm-top-10/
  5. No secrets or customer PII in public repos; turn on secret scanning + push protection. Why: GitHub Pages repos are public; anything committed is world-readable forever. Action: enable secret scanning/push protection on all repos; keep keys out of front-end code. Source: https://docs.github.com/en/code-security/secret-scanning/introduction/about-secret-scanning
  6. Lock down repo access + branch protection. Why: limits damage from a compromised collaborator/agent. Action: audit collaborators quarterly; protect main branch on smart-upkeep. Source: https://docs.github.com/en/repositories/configuring-branches-and-merges-in-your-repository/managing-protected-branches/about-protected-branches
  7. Take payments only via Stripe hosted Checkout/Elements. Why: keeps card data off your systems, minimal PCI scope (SAQ A). Action: never collect card numbers in your own forms. Source: https://docs.stripe.com/security/guide
  8. Collect less customer data; encrypt what you keep. Why: data you don't hold can't be breached. Action: list each field you collect; delete unneeded; use encrypted storage. Source: https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/protecting-personal-information-guide-business
  9. Accurate privacy policy on smartupkeep.ai. Why: FTC enforces privacy promises as deceptive practices. Action: publish a policy matching what you actually collect/share (incl. AI vendors). Source: https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/privacy-security
  10. Know Michigan breach-notice law (MCL 445.72). Why: requires notifying affected Michigan residents of qualifying breaches. Action: write a 1-page incident plan (who to call, notice template). Source: https://www.legislature.mi.gov/Laws/MCL?objectName=mcl-445-72
  11. Backups of repos + critical data, plus audit logs. Why: recovery from deletion/ransom and visibility into what agents did. Action: weekly mirror of repos to Dropbox/offline; review GitHub security log monthly. Source: https://www.cisa.gov/resources-tools/resources/cyber-essentials-toolkit ; https://docs.github.com/en/authentication/keeping-your-account-and-data-secure/reviewing-your-security-log
  12. NDAs + written IP assignment from contractors; register trademarks. Why: without assignment, contractors may own code they wrote; brand needs protection. Action: use contractor agreement with IP assignment (US + Thai helpers); search/file "Smart Upkeep" with USPTO. Source: https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks/basics ; https://www.copyright.gov/circs/circ30.pdf
